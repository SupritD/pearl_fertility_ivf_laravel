1. Irregular or Missed Periods

Occasional changes in your menstrual cycle can happen due to stress, hormonal fluctuations, or changes in weight. However, consistently irregular periods, frequently missed periods, or periods lasting longer than seven days should be evaluated by a doctor.

Conditions such as polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) and thyroid disorders can also affect menstrual cycles. A gynecologist can assess the possible causes and recommend appropriate investigations.

2. Severe Menstrual Pain

Mild cramps are common during menstruation, but pain that interferes with daily activities should not be ignored.

Severe or worsening period pain may sometimes be associated with conditions such as endometriosis, adenomyosis, or uterine fibroids. Consulting a gynecologist can help identify the cause and explore suitable treatment options.

3. Unusual Vaginal Bleeding

Bleeding between periods, after sexual intercourse, or after menopause requires medical evaluation. Although several causes are treatable, unusual bleeding should not be dismissed without understanding its cause.

If you have missed a period and experience unusual bleeding with abdominal pain, seek urgent medical assessment, as this can indicate an ectopic pregnancy.

4. Symptoms of PCOS

PCOS is a hormonal condition that may cause irregular periods, acne, increased facial hair, and difficulty conceiving.

A lady gynecologist can evaluate these symptoms, recommend relevant tests, and discuss treatment options based on individual needs. Early assessment can also help address concerns related to menstrual health and fertility.

5. Persistent Pelvic Pain

Pain or pressure in the lower abdomen that persists or repeatedly returns deserves attention. Possible causes include ovarian cysts, endometriosis, and pelvic infections.

Seek urgent medical care if the pain is sudden or severe, particularly when accompanied by fever, vomiting, faintness, or possible pregnancy.

6. Unusual Vaginal Discharge

Vaginal discharge naturally changes throughout the menstrual cycle. However, a noticeable change in colour, a strong unpleasant smell, itching, burning, or discomfort may indicate an infection or another condition.

A gynecologist can assess your symptoms and recommend appropriate treatment rather than relying on self-medication.

7. Difficulty Conceiving

Women who have concerns about conception can consult a gynecologist for an initial fertility evaluation. Assessment may include menstrual history, ovulation, medical history, and other investigations depending on individual circumstances.

If fertility treatment is needed, the doctor can explain available options, including ovulation-related treatment, intrauterine insemination (IUI), or in vitro fertilisation (IVF), where appropriate.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I visit a lady gynecologist?

Consult a gynecologist if you experience persistent menstrual irregularities, severe period pain, unusual bleeding, pelvic discomfort, or fertility concerns.

Can I visit a gynecologist without being pregnant?

Yes. Gynecologists provide care for menstrual health, hormonal concerns, reproductive health, contraception, and other conditions at different stages of life.

How often should women visit a gynecologist?

The frequency depends on your age, medical history, symptoms, and individual healthcare needs. Ask your doctor about an appropriate schedule.
